Tools

Four tools. No account. Nothing leaves your browser.

Everything runs as static pages — no server, no login, no tracking. Anything you save lives in your own browser's storage, which is also why you should export what you care about.

Assessment

Liability vs. Asset

Two analyses in one worksheet. First the risk structure — likelihood of failure, likelihood of impacting a target, consequences — rendered through the published matrix so you can see exactly which cell you landed in and why.

Then a value ledger: condition, remaining useful life, canopy contribution, functions delivered, structural defects, maintenance burden, infrastructure conflict, and whether the species itself is the problem. You get a net verdict, both columns itemized, and a prioritized action list.

Selection

Planting Guide by ZIP

A ZIP code resolves to an approximate hardiness zone, planting region and moisture regime. Describe the site — overhead lines, planting strip width, drainage, soil pH, salt exposure, setback from structures, light — and every species in the database gets scored against it.

Results carry the named cultivars to specify, not just genus and species. And a "do not plant here" list, because knowing what to refuse is half the job.

Study

ISA Study Guide

Thirteen knowledge domains with key points and terminology, 90 flashcards, and a 79-question practice bank where every answer carries an explanation. Answer choices are shuffled on every session so you cannot learn the position instead of the content.

Sessions are scored by domain, so the result tells you where to spend the next hour rather than just handing you a percentage.

Field

TreePlot GPS

Walk a property with a phone and drop precise GPS points — species, DBH, condition, risk rating, notes — with the accuracy radius shown so you know what the fix is worth. Or tap the map to place a tree by eye.

Live 10-20-30 diversity checking on whatever you have inventoried, and export to GeoJSON or CSV so the data goes straight into a real GIS instead of dying in an app.

How the pieces fit

The four tools answer the same question at four points in a tree's life. TreePlot records what is there. Liability vs. Asset decides what each one is worth keeping. Planting Guide decides what replaces the ones that go, and what to put in the empty spots. Study Guide is how the person making those calls gets qualified to make them.

Data portability is deliberate. TreePlot exports GeoJSON and CSV. The assessment prints to PDF through your browser. Nothing here holds your records hostage.

These tools structure a judgment. They do not make one. No web form can see a root plate lifting, sound a hollow stem with a mallet, or read the grade change a builder made fifteen years ago. Use them to organize what you know and to argue with your own conclusions — then have a qualified arborist look at the tree.
