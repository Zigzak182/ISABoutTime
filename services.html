Services

What we actually do

Five service lines, all built on the same question: what does this tree return, what does it cost, and what changes that balance?

01

Tree Risk Assessment

A Level 2 basic assessment: a systematic 360-degree ground inspection of the tree, the root collar and the site. We rate likelihood of failure, likelihood of impacting a target, and consequences — and give you a rating with a stated time frame, because a rating without one is not defensible.

Where the ground inspection raises a question it cannot answer — how much sound wood is left in a stem with a cavity — we advance to Level 3 tools rather than guessing.

You get: a written report, a rating per tree, mitigation options ranked by cost, and a re-inspection interval.

Common triggers: a storm on the street, a real estate transaction, an insurer's letter, a limb that came down.

02

Pruning & Structure

Pruned to ANSI A300, with a stated objective before the saw comes out. Cleaning, thinning, raising, reduction and restoration — and nothing else. We do not top trees, and we will explain at length why, if you want.

The highest-value work we sell is structural pruning on young trees: establishing one dominant leader and spacing the scaffolds. Ten minutes of work at year three prevents the codominant failure that needs a crane at year thirty.

You get: correct cuts at the branch collar, a stated dose within what the tree can afford, and a photo record.

If someone quoted you "topping" or "hat-racking," get a second opinion.

03

Plant Health Care & Diagnosis

We work the pattern before naming a culprit. Damage spread uniformly across many species points to something environmental — herbicide, salt, grade change, drought. Damage confined to one species and progressing over time points to a pathogen or insect.

Most urban tree problems turn out to be abiotic, and most borers and canker fungi are secondary to a stress nobody diagnosed. We excavate the root flare on every decline call, because that is where the answer usually is.

You get: a diagnosis with reasoning, soil testing where it changes the answer, and an IPM plan — cultural first, chemical last and narrowly targeted.

We will tell you when a problem is cosmetic and needs nothing.

04

Planting & Species Selection

Right tree, right place, specified by cultivar. There is a real difference between "a ginkgo" and a named male ginkgo cultivar, and the difference shows up as fruit odor for fifty years if nobody writes it down.

We plant at the correct depth with the root flare visible, correct circling roots at planting, backfill with native soil, and mulch wide and off the trunk. Then we tell you honestly how long the irrigation obligation lasts — about a year per inch of caliper.

You get: a species and cultivar specification you can hand to any nursery, correct installation, and an establishment watering schedule.

Try the ZIP code planting guide to see how the selection logic works.

05

Construction Tree Protection

Construction kills more established trees than any pest, and it kills them slowly enough that nobody connects the two. Roots get severed, soil gets compacted by traffic and storage, and grade gets changed over the root zone. The decline shows up three to seven years later, long after the contractor's warranty ended.

We inventory and assess before the fence goes up, tell you honestly which trees are worth protecting, specify the tree protection zone, and write it into the contract with a dollar penalty. Trees preserved by handshake do not survive. Where utilities have to cross a root zone, we specify boring rather than trenching — that one decision alone often decides whether a mature tree lives.

You get: a pre-construction inventory, TPZ specifications and fencing, root pruning done cleanly with an air excavation tool, monitoring during the build, and multi-year aftercare.

What we will not do

  • Top a tree. It removes the canopy that feeds the tree, opens wounds too large to compartmentalize, and produces a mass of weakly attached sprouts. The result is a more hazardous, shorter-lived tree at higher long-term cost.
  • Lion's-tail a canopy. Stripping interior foliage moves weight to the branch tips, increases wind loading and exposes bark to sunscald.
  • Cable a tree that should come down. Hardware supplements sound structure. On a failing tree it transfers liability without reducing risk.
  • Sell fertilizer as a cure. It does nothing for compaction, girdling roots, planting depth or borers — which is most of what people call us about.
  • Recommend a tree we would not plant at our own house. Callery pear, silver maple, Leyland cypress in a tight row, and Bradford anything.
