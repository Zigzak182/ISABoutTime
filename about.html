About

What Seed is, and what it isn't

Four browser tools for arboriculture, free and open to anyone. No company behind the recommendations, no account, no server, and no analytics on the tools.

Who it's for

Working arborists who want a structured worksheet instead of a gut call. Candidates studying for certification. Urban foresters managing a population rather than a tree. Landscape architects specifying plantings that have to survive the site. And property owners trying to make an honest decision about a tree somebody just quoted them $3,000 to remove.

None of the tools require an account, a subscription, or a tree care company.

Standards it's built on

ISA tree risk assessment framework
The likelihood of failure / likelihood of impact / consequences structure, implemented as both published matrices.
ANSI A300
The performance standard for tree care operations — pruning types, support systems, management. Referenced throughout the reference notes and study guide.
ANSI Z133
The safety standard for arboricultural operations: electrical clearances, drop zones, PPE, two points of attachment aloft, aerial rescue.
USDA plant hardiness zones
The 2023 map, approximated to ZIP prefix. See the limits below.

How it works

Seed is a static site. There is no backend, no database and no account system. The species database, the ZIP-to-zone table and the study content ship as plain files that load with the page, which is why the tools keep working with no connection.

Anything you save — plotted trees, study progress, quiz history — lives in your own browser's storage and goes nowhere else. That is a privacy property and a data-loss risk in equal measure, which is why TreePlot exports GeoJSON and CSV and why you should use it at the end of every site visit.

Install it to a phone home screen and it runs offline, including the map, if you save the area before leaving signal.

Limits worth stating plainly

These tools structure a judgment. They do not make one. No web form can see a root plate lifting, sound a hollow stem with a mallet, read the grade change a builder made fifteen years ago, or notice the conk hiding under the ivy. A Level 2 basic assessment by a qualified arborist is what a rating should be built on. Seed organizes what you already observed; it is not a substitute for observing it.
ZIP code is a climate filter, not a site assessment. A ZIP3 prefix covers a wide area. Elevation, urban heat island, cold-air drainage and large water bodies shift the real hardiness zone by half a zone or more, and soil pH cannot be predicted from a ZIP code at all. Confirm on site, and soil-test before anything expensive goes in the ground.
The species database is curated, not exhaustive. 79 taxa chosen to cover the major US regions and, deliberately, the common liability trees. It is not a substitute for regional extension guidance, and it is not a legal authority — always check your state's current invasive species list before specifying anything.
The study material is original and is not ISA content. It is organized around the ISA Certified Arborist knowledge domains, but it is not produced, endorsed by, or affiliated with the International Society of Arboriculture, and it is not a substitute for the official study guide, the BMP series, or supervised field experience. The domain percentages shown are this project's study-time guidance, not exam weightings.

Found something wrong?

Species data, zone boundaries and practice notes all benefit from people who work in a region correcting them.
