Credentials, standards, and how we think

Tree care is one of the few trades where the consequences of bad work take twenty years to appear. Standards exist so that gap does not get exploited.

ISA Certified Arborist

Certification through the International Society of Arboriculture requires documented field experience, a proctored exam across thirteen knowledge domains, and continuing education units to maintain. It is the baseline credential for anyone giving advice about a tree you are going to keep.

Before you publish: replace this block with the certifying arborist's name and certification number so clients can verify it directly in ISA's public "Verify a Credential" directory. An unverifiable claim is worth less than no claim.

Standards we work to

ANSI A300
The performance standard for tree care operations — pruning, support systems, lightning protection, management and more. Every pruning job starts with a stated objective under Part 1.
ANSI Z133
The safety standard for arboricultural operations: electrical clearances, drop zones, PPE, two points of attachment aloft, and aerial rescue capability on site.
ISA Best Management Practices
The applied companion to the standards, covering risk assessment, pruning, soil management, tree protection and integrated pest management.

Why we built the platform

Most of what an arborist knows is not secret. It is just unevenly distributed. A homeowner deciding whether to spend $3,000 on a removal has no structured way to think about it, so the decision gets made on whoever sounded most confident in the driveway.

The tools on this site are the same structure we use internally, opened up. The risk matrix is the ISA framework. The species database carries the specific cultivar names that separate a good specification from a vague one. The study guide is there because the industry needs more certified arborists, not fewer.

Everything runs in your browser. There is no account, no server, and no analytics on the tools. Your plotted trees and study progress live in your own browser storage and go nowhere — which also means you should export anything you care about.

On the study material: it is written specifically for this platform. It is organized around the ISA Certified Arborist knowledge domains, but it is not ISA content, is not endorsed by or affiliated with ISA, and is not a substitute for the official study guide or for supervised field experience. Use it as a drill, alongside the real thing.
On the tools: the assessment and planting tools are decision support. They structure a judgment; they do not make one. No web form can see a root plate lifting, hear a hollow stem, or read the grade change a builder made in 2009. Have a qualified arborist look at the tree.
