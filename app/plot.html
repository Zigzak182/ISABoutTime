Accuracy, honestly

A phone's GPS is typically good to somewhere between 3 and 10 metres under open sky, and considerably worse under a closed canopy or between buildings — which is exactly where street trees live. The accuracy radius is recorded with every point and exported in the data, so downstream users can see what a fix was worth.

For survey-grade positions you want an external RTK or DGPS receiver feeding the device. For an inventory where the goal is "find this tree again and know which one it is," phone GPS plus a species and DBH note is genuinely enough.

Where your data lives

In this browser, on this device, in localStorage. It is not uploaded anywhere and there is no account behind it. That is a privacy feature and a data-loss risk in equal measure.

Export at the end of every site visit. Clearing site data, switching browsers or using a private window will lose the inventory. GeoJSON opens directly in QGIS or ArcGIS; CSV opens anywhere.
