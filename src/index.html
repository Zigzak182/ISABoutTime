Why it is built this way

The tree is not the subject. The site is.

A tree fails for one of three reasons: it was the wrong species for the site, it was never given structure when structure was cheap to build, or something happened to its roots that nobody saw. Almost every expensive tree problem is one of those three, decided years before the failure.

So the tools start below ground. The assessment prompts for root flare condition and girdling roots, because those cause more landscape tree decline than every insect and disease combined, and neither is visible from the canopy. The planting guide weights soil pH, drainage and compaction above ornamental preference. The species database records a liability case for every taxon, including the good ones.

None of this is secret knowledge. It is just unevenly distributed, and a homeowner deciding whether to spend $3,000 on a removal usually has no structured way to think about it. Seed is an attempt to hand that structure to whoever needs it.
