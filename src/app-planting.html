How this works

The ZIP prefix resolves to an approximate USDA hardiness zone, a planting region and a moisture regime. Every species in the database is then scored against your site: hardiness margin, regional track record, mature size against overhead lines and setback, rooting width, drainage, pH, salt and compaction tolerance, light, purpose, and longevity.

Anything that fails a hard constraint is excluded and the reason is stated. Everything else is ranked, and every card can be expanded to show exactly which factors moved its score.

Results carry named cultivars where they matter — a male ginkgo, a fruitless sweetgum, a narrow oak that fits an 8 ft strip. Handing a nursery a genus is how a client ends up with a fruiting female ginkgo for the next fifty years.

A ZIP is a starting filter, not a site assessment. Elevation, urban heat island, cold-air drainage and large water bodies all shift the real zone by half a zone or more, and soil pH cannot be predicted from a ZIP code at all. Confirm on site, and soil-test before anything expensive goes in the ground.
